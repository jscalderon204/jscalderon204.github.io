# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences read this portfolio at once, and it must serve both without dumbing down for either:

- **Recruiters and hiring managers (primary).** Skimming fast, one tab among many, deciding in seconds. The value and the proof of high-level work must land before any deep reading.
- **Technical peers (DS / ML / research).** Reading deeply, judging rigor, method, and the honesty of the numbers. They are rewarded on a deep read, not gated at the top, and they will check the claims.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for **Joseph Calderon**, a San Diego data scientist, built to **land interviews and establish credibility**. Success is a recruiter forwarding the link and a skeptical peer trusting the numbers. The site presents three projects in priority order (the flagship Qwen-4B math-reasoning work, the SoCalGuessr CNN, and the Sea Ice scrollytelling piece), plus the person behind them, with a clear way to make contact.

## Brand Personality

Sharp, technical, and measured. Authority comes from clarity and substantiated claims, never from buzzwords. The voice is calibrated to the author's own writing sample: complete, flowing sentences with reflective connective tissue, low on contractions, drawn to the pleasure of a hard, constrained problem. Confident without performing confidence. It should read as the work of a real, capable person, not a template.

## Anti-references

- The dark-terminal "ML = neon-on-near-black" landing page, and its glow.
- Gradient or mesh hero backgrounds, gradient text, glassmorphism.
- The hero-metric SaaS template (big number, small label, decorative stats).
- Editorial-magazine affectation (display-serif italic, drop caps, ruled-column broadsheet) on a brief that is not a magazine.
- Marketing buzzwords (streamline, leverage, supercharge, seamless) and aphoristic "statement, then punchy negation" cadence.
- Invented or rounded-up metrics presented as fact. Any visual lifted from the source slide deck.
- Em dashes (and `--`). Use commas, colons, semicolons, periods, or parentheses.

## Design Principles

- **Measured truth, made visible.** Every number traces to a real source file, and the provenance is shown on the figure. This honesty is the credibility, and it is the site's signature.
- **The skim is a true subset of the depth.** One set of true claims shown at two resolutions, so the peer's deep read confirms the skimmer's first impression instead of contradicting it.
- **Distinguish measured from illustrative, always.** A single redundantly-encoded token (color, type, marker, label) that survives grayscale and colorblindness. The Sea Ice project carries no metric by design.
- **Constraints are the interesting part.** The work and the voice both treat limits (a model you cannot retrain, a tiny net, one GPU) as the point, not an apology.
- **Rigor made legible.** Substantiated depth for the peer, instant proof for the skimmer, without dumbing down either.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA or better on all text and controls. Nothing depends on color alone: the measured/illustrative system and every chart carry label, pattern, and direct value, never hue only. Charts ship a `<table>` data equivalent and `<title>`/`<desc>`. Full keyboard reach with visible focus, semantic landmarks, and a complete `prefers-reduced-motion` path (charts render fully without animation; the Sea Ice piece settles to a static end state and the showcase video does not autoplay).
